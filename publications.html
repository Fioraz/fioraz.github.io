A selection of my research work, from air‑quality monitoring with IoT and machine learning to forensic analysis of widely used financial mobile applications.

Unveiling Air Visio: A Ground‑breaking Exploration in Air Quality Monitoring

Air Visio is a smart air‑quality monitoring system I designed and led as a research project, later published at the 2019 IEEE ICAC conference.

Using an Arduino‑based IoT device and an Android app, the system measures local air quality in real time, visualises it for users, and even predicts pollution levels across the country. It can also suggest the cleanest travel route using interactive maps, helping people reduce their exposure to polluted areas.

This project brought together my skills in programming, data collection, analysis, machine learning and team leadership, and reflects my passion for using technology to support healthier, more sustainable living.

MSc Dissertation: Forensic Analysis of Credit Scoring Mobile Applications

(Research completed and available online; not yet formally published in a journal.)

Credit scoring apps like ClearScore, Credit Karma and Experian are widely used in the UK to quickly check credit scores, credit reports and eligibility for loans and credit cards. However, these apps handle a large amount of sensitive personal data, and there has been little research into how safely this data is stored on phones.

In my Master’s research, I examined these three apps on both Android and iOS to see what information they leave behind, even after being uninstalled. I was able to recover personal details such as names, addresses, phone numbers and email addresses from the devices.

These findings show that, despite built‑in security features, private data can still remain on a phone and potentially be exposed—raising important questions about privacy and security for everyday users.
